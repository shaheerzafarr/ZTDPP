02   THE PROBLEM AND OUR ANSWER

Each weakness in digital media needs a different kind of evidence.

Real-world problemZTDPP answer
A file can be edited after it leaves its creator.Versioned manifests, parent-child lineage, and revocation preserve the history.
Metadata can be removed or changed.Metadata is treated as evidence, then cross-checked against hashes, signatures, and ledger records.
The same image may be resized or recompressed.Perceptual hashes help find visually similar registered content.
AI-generated media may have no trusted origin record.The AI service provides a pixel-based probability as a supporting signal.
A single score can be misleading.The trust report includes the score breakdown, signals, matched claim, and verification checks.

Our key design decision

We do not claim that AI detection alone proves whether an image is real or fake. AI models can fail on new generators or edited files. ZTDPP combines independent evidence and clearly reports when evidence is missing, conflicting, or unavailable.

Defense point
If asked, “Why not just use the AI model?”, answer: provenance proves history, while AI estimates from visual patterns. They solve different parts of the problem.

03   HOW ONE IMAGE MOVES THROUGH ZTDPP

The complete flow can be explained in ten simple steps.

  1. A creator or authorized user registers an image or a privacy-preserving content hash.
  2. The backend validates it and extracts SHA-256, perceptual hashes, and available metadata.
  3. The platform creates a canonical manifest describing content, source, action, ownership, and evidence.
  4. The manifest is signed with the platform Ed25519 key and added to the provenance ledger.
  5. If the image is edited, the new version points to its parent so the full lineage stays visible.
  6. During verification, uploaded bytes remain in memory while hashes and metadata are extracted.
  7. The backend searches for an exact claim, then tries a perceptual near match.
  8. At the same time, the backend calls the FastAPI AI service for a pixel-based prediction.
  9. The trust engine combines the available evidence, applies safety caps, and produces a verdict.
  10. The complete report is saved with its evidence and policy snapshot for later review.

What is returned?

Response
Verdict, classification, trust score, metadata score, AI score, effective weights, evidence signals, matched provenance, signature and ledger checks, input metadata, and processing time.

Why is AI a separate service?

  • Python and PyTorch are the natural environment for image inference.
  • The core platform can degrade safely if AI times out or becomes unavailable.
  • A future model can be deployed without rewriting the frontend or provenance platform.

06   SOHAIB — BACKEND DEFENSE

The backend is the trust boundary of the platform.

  • Identity: OTP activation, password hashing, JWT cookies, refresh-session rotation, logout, and role guards.
  • Provenance: SHA-256, pHash/dHash, metadata, signed manifests, ownership, edits, revocation, and lineage.
  • Ledger: ordered entries, previous links, Merkle roots/proofs, signed blocks, and integrity verification.
  • Orchestration: metadata/hash processing and AI calls run together; exact and near-match claims are resolved.
  • Trust engine: combines evidence, redistributes unavailable weights, and applies safety caps.
  • Developer API: peppered key hashes, scopes, rate limits, usage, rotation, and revocation.

Suggested speaking script

Sohaib says“The backend never accepts a stored claim without checking it again. It recomputes hashes, validates the manifest signature, checks the ledger and Merkle proof, calls the AI service, and gives the trust engine structured evidence. MongoDB stores evidence and history, while normal verification bytes are processed in memory and discarded.”
Say this
The ledger is tamper-evident and platform-operated. It is a signed hash chain with Merkle proofs; we should not oversell it as a public decentralized blockchain.

07   SHAHEER — AI DEFENSE

The AI model is a supporting forensic signal, not absolute proof.

  • Data pipeline: validation, metadata ingestion, content hashing, balanced sampling, and split checks.
  • Model: ConvNeXt Tiny plus a residual/high-pass stream for forensic texture cues.
  • Training evidence: 94.84% validation accuracy, 95.67% precision, 93.93% recall, and 94.79% F1.
  • Service: FastAPI loads best.pt, applies trained preprocessing, and exposes health and prediction endpoints.
  • Integration: timeouts, API-key protection, health probes, and controlled fallback.
  • Known limitation: a watermark-removed Gemini sample produced a severe false negative.

Suggested speaking script

Shaheer says“The model analyzes pixel patterns that may indicate synthetic generation. It performed strongly on its validation set, but validation accuracy is not universal reliability. We found a difficult Gemini image that the model missed, so ZTDPP reports AI probability as evidence rather than absolute proof.”

If asked why the model made a mistake

Answer
The fingerprints learned from training generators may not appear in a new generator or may be weakened by editing, compression, resizing, or watermark removal. The correct response is broader training and generator-held-out testing—not simply lowering the threshold.

08   TRUST SCORE AND VERDICT

The score combines evidence, but critical failures still control the verdict.

The default policy gives 60% importance to provenance/metadata and 40% to AI. If one signal is unavailable, its weight is redistributed instead of silently treating missing evidence as perfect.

EvidenceWhat increases confidenceWhat reduces confidence
ProvenanceExact or clear near match, valid signature and ledger proof, active claimNo claim, ambiguity, revocation, broken signature, or ledger failure
MetadataConsistent source/action information and expected fieldsMissing, contradictory, suspicious metadata or generator hints
AILow calibrated AI-generated probabilityHigh probability, disagreement, or unavailable model

Verdict levels

VerdictEasy meaning
TrustedStrong verified provenance and no critical integrity failure.
Likely authenticEvidence mostly supports authenticity, but confidence is below Trusted.
SuspiciousImportant evidence is missing, weak, or conflicting.
UntrustedA critical integrity failure, revocation, or strong negative evidence is present.
Key point
Hard safety caps stop a high average score from hiding a critical failure such as a broken signature or revoked provenance.

11   LIKELY DEFENSE QUESTIONS

Answer directly, then add technical detail only if asked.

QuestionStrong short answer
What is the main novelty?Combining signed provenance, tamper-evident lineage, perceptual matching, metadata, AI evidence, and an explainable trust policy.
Is the ledger a blockchain?It is a platform-operated signed hash chain with Merkle proofs, not a decentralized public blockchain.
Can AI prove an image is fake?No. AI provides a probability. ZTDPP combines it with independent provenance and integrity evidence.
Why MongoDB?The domain contains evolving nested evidence, manifests, lineage, reports, and settings; MongoDB supports that structure and transaction-backed writes.
What if AI is down?The backend reports AI as unavailable, redistributes effective weights, and does not invent a result.
How is tampering detected?By recomputing hashes, Ed25519 signatures, previous-entry links, Merkle proofs, and block integrity.
Do you store verification images?Normal verification processes bytes in memory and saves evidence and results, not the image content.
What is required before production?Authorization closure, production secrets, recovery proof, E2E/load tests, and generator-held-out AI gates.
